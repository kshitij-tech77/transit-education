## 1. United Kingdom

**Page:** `/uk/` · **Post ID:** 1874 · **Slug:** `uk`

### H1 — Study in the UK

### H2 — Overview

The United Kingdom hosts four of the world's top ten universities and a higher-education tradition that goes back nearly a thousand years. For Nepalese students, the UK offers internationally recognised degrees, shorter programme lengths than most other destinations (three-year bachelor's and one-year master's), an English-speaking environment, and a Graduate Route post-study work visa that lets graduates stay and work for up to two years (three for PhD graduates) without employer sponsorship. Nepali alumni networks are well established at the top universities, and direct flights from Kathmandu to London via the Gulf make travel straightforward.

### H2 — Why Study in the UK

- **Globally ranked institutions** — Oxford, Cambridge, Imperial College, UCL, and Edinburgh are consistently in the QS top 25.
- **Shorter degrees, lower total cost** — a UK master's takes one year vs two in the USA or Canada.
- **Graduate Route visa** — two years of post-study work for any graduate, three years for PhDs, no job offer required.
- **Strong industry links** — internships, placements, and sandwich years built into many programmes.
- **Multicultural campuses** — UK universities host more than 600,000 international students, including a sizeable Nepali community.

### H3 — Major Intakes

UK universities run two main intakes — **September/October** (the primary intake, with the widest course choice) and **January/February** (limited courses, primarily at post-1992 universities and some Russell Group programmes). A small **May intake** exists at a handful of universities for selected business and computing programmes. Apply at least 6–8 months before your intended intake; popular courses fill fast.

### H3 — Entry Requirements

**Undergraduate (Bachelor's)**

- Minimum 60–70% in 12-year schooling (NEB +2 / A-Levels equivalent).
- IELTS 6.0–6.5 overall (no band below 5.5–6.0), or accepted equivalents (PTE 50–62, TOEFL iBT 79–90).
- UCAS personal statement and one academic reference.

**Postgraduate (Master's / PhD)**

- Bachelor's degree from a recognised university with at least 50–60% (2:2 or 2:1 UK equivalent).
- IELTS 6.5 overall (no band below 6.0); some Russell Group programmes require 7.0.
- Two academic / professional references and a Statement of Purpose.
- GMAT/GRE may be required for top business and STEM master's programmes.

### H3 — Top Universities in the UK

University of Oxford · University of Cambridge · Imperial College London · UCL · University of Edinburgh · King's College London · University of Manchester · LSE · University of Bristol · University of Warwick · University of Glasgow · Durham University · University of Southampton · University of Birmingham · University of Leeds · University of Sheffield · Queen Mary University of London · University of Nottingham · Newcastle University · Cardiff University · University of Liverpool · University of Exeter · Lancaster University · University of York · University of East Anglia · Coventry University · University of Greenwich · Middlesex University · University of Hertfordshire · Brunel University London.

### H3 — UK Student Visa Process for Nepalese Students

1. **Receive your Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS).** After accepting an unconditional offer and paying any required deposit, your university issues a CAS letter — a unique reference number you'll use to apply for the visa.
2. **Apply for No Objection Certificate (NOC).** Apply online at noc.moest.gov.np with scanned copies of your academic and CAS documents. Equivalence certificates from CDC Bhaktapur or Tribhuvan University are required for foreign-board qualifications.
3. **Pay your tuition fee deposit** and obtain the receipt — required as part of financial evidence.
4. **Prepare financial evidence.** Show 28 consecutive days of bank balance covering tuition (year one) plus living costs (currently £1,334/month inside London, £1,023/month outside London for up to 9 months).
5. **Book and complete the TB test** at IOM Kathmandu (mandatory for visa applicants from Nepal).
6. **Submit the Student Route visa application** online at gov.uk/student-visa, pay the visa fee (currently £490) and the Immigration Health Surcharge (£776/year).
7. **Biometrics appointment at VFS Kathmandu** (Chhaya Complex, Thamel). Carry your passport, appointment letter, CAS, and financial documents.
8. **Decision** — usually within 3 weeks. Collect your passport with vignette from VFS, then your Biometric Residence Permit on arrival in the UK.

### H3 — Frequently Asked Questions about Studying in the UK

**Q: Can international students work part-time during their studies in the UK?**
A: Yes. Student visa holders can work up to 20 hours per week during term time and full-time during scheduled vacations, provided the course is at degree level at a UK Higher Education Provider with track record.

**Q: What is the Graduate Route visa and how long can I stay after my degree?**
A: The Graduate Route is a post-study work visa that lets bachelor's and master's graduates stay in the UK for two years after completing their course (three years for PhD graduates) without needing a job offer or sponsorship. You apply from inside the UK before your Student visa expires.

**Q: How much money do I need to show for a UK student visa from Nepal?**
A: You must show one academic year of tuition (or the unpaid balance) plus living costs of £1,334/month for London or £1,023/month for outside London, for up to nine months. The funds must sit in a bank account in your name, your parent's, or your legal guardian's for 28 consecutive days before the application.

## 2. United States of America

**Page:** `/usa/` · **Post ID:** 1878 · **Slug:** `usa`

### H1 — Study in the USA

### H2 — Overview

The United States is the world's largest higher-education ecosystem, with more than 4,000 accredited universities and the broadest range of programmes anywhere — from liberal arts colleges to specialised engineering institutes and global research universities. For Nepalese students, the USA offers world-leading research facilities, generous merit and need-based scholarships, the chance to switch majors mid-degree, paid Optional Practical Training (OPT) for one year after graduation (extended by 24 months for STEM fields), and direct access to one of the largest job markets in the world.

### H2 — Why Study in the USA

- **Top-ranked universities** — Harvard, MIT, Stanford, Caltech, Princeton, Yale, and Columbia consistently lead global rankings.
- **OPT and STEM OPT** — up to 36 months of post-study work for STEM graduates without employer sponsorship.
- **Generous funding** — assistantships, fellowships, and merit scholarships, especially at the master's and PhD level.
- **Flexibility** — declare or change majors after enrolment; build interdisciplinary degrees.
- **On-campus work** — up to 20 hours/week during term and 40 hours in vacations on F-1 status.

### H3 — Major Intakes

US universities run three intakes — **Fall (August/September)** is the main intake with the widest course catalogue and most funding; **Spring (January)** has a more limited but still substantial selection; **Summer (May)** is small and mainly used for graduate research starts. Apply 9–12 months before intake. Standardised tests (SAT/ACT for undergraduate, GRE/GMAT for graduate) are still required at many top institutions despite recent test-optional trends.

### H3 — Entry Requirements

**Undergraduate**

- Minimum GPA equivalent of 3.0/4.0 (≈ 70% in NEB) or higher for top-tier universities.
- TOEFL iBT 79–100, IELTS 6.5–7.5, or Duolingo English Test 105+.
- SAT 1200+ or ACT 25+ (test-optional at many universities, but submitting strong scores helps).
- Common App essay, two academic references, and a counsellor recommendation.

**Postgraduate**

- Bachelor's degree (16 years of education) with GPA 3.0+; some programmes accept 15 years with bridge work.
- TOEFL iBT 90–100 or IELTS 6.5–7.5.
- GRE 300+ for STEM, GMAT 600+ for business.
- Statement of Purpose, three academic/professional recommendations, and CV.

### H3 — Top Universities in the USA

Harvard University · Stanford University · MIT · Princeton University · Yale University · Columbia University · University of Chicago · UC Berkeley · UCLA · University of Pennsylvania · Cornell University · Duke University · University of Michigan – Ann Arbor · Northwestern University · Carnegie Mellon University · New York University · University of Southern California · Georgia Tech · University of Texas at Austin · University of Wisconsin–Madison · University of Illinois Urbana–Champaign · University of Washington · Boston University · Purdue University · Arizona State University · Texas A&M · University of Florida · George Mason University · University of North Texas · Pace University.

### H3 — USA Student Visa (F-1) Process for Nepalese Students

1. **Apply and get admitted** — receive your I-20 form from a SEVP-certified university.
2. **Apply for NOC** at noc.moest.gov.np with your I-20, academic records, and test scores.
3. **Pay the SEVIS I-901 fee** ($350 currently) at fmjfee.com and keep the receipt.
4. **Complete DS-160** non-immigrant visa application online; upload a US-spec photo (2x2 inches, white background).
5. **Pay the visa application fee** (MRV, currently $185) at the Standard Chartered Bank counter.
6. **Schedule two appointments** at the US Embassy in Kathmandu (Maharajgunj) — one for biometrics, one for the visa interview. Use ustraveldocs.com.
7. **Attend the interview.** Carry passport, I-20, SEVIS receipt, DS-160 confirmation, financial documents (bank balance, sponsor affidavit, scholarship letter), academic transcripts, and test scores.
8. **Visa approval and travel.** If approved, your passport with the F-1 visa is delivered within 5–10 working days. Enter the US no earlier than 30 days before your I-20 start date.

### H3 — Frequently Asked Questions about Studying in the USA

**Q: Can F-1 students work in the USA?**
A: Yes — on-campus jobs up to 20 hours per week during term and 40 hours during vacations, no separate work permit needed. Off-campus work requires CPT (during studies) or OPT (post-graduation), both authorised through your university's international student office.

**Q: What is OPT and STEM OPT?**
A: OPT (Optional Practical Training) is 12 months of post-graduation work authorisation in your field of study. If your degree is in a Department of Homeland Security-listed STEM field, you can apply for a 24-month STEM OPT extension, totalling 36 months of post-study work.

**Q: How much funding do I need to show for an F-1 visa from Nepal?**
A: You must demonstrate enough funds to cover the first year's total cost of attendance shown on your I-20 — typically $35,000–$80,000 depending on the university. Funds can be a mix of personal/family bank balance, education loan, scholarship, and assistantship offers.

## 3. Australia

**Page:** `/australia/` · **Post ID:** 1872 · **Slug:** `australia`

### H1 — Study in Australia

### H2 — Overview

Australia hosts six universities in the QS world top 50 and is one of the most welcoming destinations for international students, with English-medium instruction, work rights of 48 hours per fortnight during studies, a Temporary Graduate visa (subclass 485) that allows two to four years of post-study work depending on the qualification, and a strong system of student protections under the ESOS Act. For Nepalese students, Australia is one of the most popular destinations after the USA and Canada — cities like Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, and Adelaide have established Nepali communities and direct flight connections.

### H2 — Why Study in Australia

- **Top-ranked universities** — University of Melbourne, ANU, University of Sydney, UNSW, University of Queensland, and Monash are all in the QS top 50.
- **Post-study work visa** — 2 years for bachelor's, 3 years for master's coursework, 4 years for master's research and PhD.
- **Strong work rights** — 48 hours per fortnight during term, unlimited during scheduled breaks.
- **High-quality regulation** — Australian Qualifications Framework and ESOS Act protect international students by law.
- **Pathways to permanent residency** — many qualifications align with the Skilled Occupation List.

### H3 — Major Intakes

Australia has two major intakes — **February** (the primary intake with the widest course choice) and **July** (a strong secondary intake, especially for postgraduate programmes). Some institutions also offer a **November** intake for selected courses. Apply 4–6 months before your intended start date. Genuine Temporary Entrant (GTE) requirements were replaced in 2024 with the new **Genuine Student (GS) test** — be ready to demonstrate clear study purpose in writing.

### H3 — Entry Requirements

**Undergraduate (Bachelor's)**

- Minimum 60% in 12-year schooling (NEB +2 or equivalent).
- IELTS 6.0 overall with no band below 5.5 (6.5 with no band below 6.0 for top universities).
- Genuine Student (GS) statement.

**Postgraduate**

- Bachelor's degree from a recognised university, typically 60%+ aggregate.
- IELTS 6.5 overall with no band below 6.0 (7.0 for some professional programmes like Education and Nursing).
- Two academic references and a Statement of Purpose.

### H3 — Top Universities in Australia

University of Melbourne · Australian National University (ANU) · University of Sydney · University of New South Wales (UNSW) · University of Queensland · Monash University · University of Western Australia · University of Adelaide · University of Technology Sydney (UTS) · Macquarie University · University of Wollongong · Queensland University of Technology · RMIT University · Deakin University · La Trobe University · Curtin University · Griffith University · University of Newcastle · Flinders University · Western Sydney University · University of South Australia · University of Tasmania · Federation University Australia · Charles Sturt University · Edith Cowan University · Murdoch University · Bond University · Australian Catholic University · James Cook University · Victoria University.

### H3 — Australia Student Visa (Subclass 500) Process for Nepalese Students

1. **Receive your Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE)** from a CRICOS-registered provider after accepting your offer and paying the initial tuition deposit.
2. **Apply for NOC** at noc.moest.gov.np.
3. **Get Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC)** from a recognised provider — mandatory for the entire visa duration.
4. **Prepare your Genuine Student (GS) statement** — a written submission explaining your study choice, future plans, and ties to Nepal.
5. **Complete the medical examination** at IOM Kathmandu through eMedical (results uploaded directly to Department of Home Affairs).
6. **Lodge your visa application** online via ImmiAccount with CoE, OSHC, GS statement, financial proof, English test results, and medical reference.
7. **Pay the visa fee** (currently AUD 1,600).
8. **Biometrics submission** at VFS Kathmandu after receiving the request letter.
9. **Decision** — typical processing time is 4–8 weeks. Approved visas are issued electronically (no sticker on passport).

### H3 — Frequently Asked Questions about Studying in Australia

**Q: How many hours can international students work in Australia?**
A: Student visa holders can work up to 48 hours per fortnight (every two weeks) during teaching periods and unlimited hours during scheduled course breaks. The fortnightly cap allows for some flexibility week to week as long as the two-week total is within the limit.

**Q: What is the Temporary Graduate visa and how long can I stay after graduation?**
A: The subclass 485 Temporary Graduate visa lets you stay and work in Australia after completing your course — 2 years for a bachelor's, 3 years for a master's by coursework, 4 years for master's by research, and up to 6 years for a PhD. You must apply within 6 months of completing your studies.

**Q: What is OSHC and is it mandatory?**
A: Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC) is health insurance that international students must hold for the entire duration of their student visa. It covers doctor visits, hospital stays, ambulance, and most prescription medicines. Major providers include Bupa, Medibank, Allianz Care, and nib.

## 4. New Zealand

**Page:** `/new-zealand/` · **Post ID:** 1876 · **Slug:** `new-zealand`

### H1 — Study in New Zealand

### H2 — Overview

New Zealand offers a British-system education within a small, safe, and welcoming country — all eight of its universities sit in the QS world top 500, and the country consistently ranks among the safest in the world. For Nepalese students, New Zealand offers a Post-Study Work Visa of one to three years depending on the qualification and study location, an English-medium environment, comparatively lower living costs than Australia or the UK, and pathways to skilled migration through the Green List of in-demand occupations.

### H2 — Why Study in New Zealand

- **All 8 universities in the QS top 500** — high baseline quality across the system.
- **Post-Study Work Visa** — up to 3 years to live and work in NZ after qualifying study.
- **Pathways to residency** — Green List occupations enable Skilled Migrant Category applications.
- **Safe and welcoming** — consistently ranked top 5 in the Global Peace Index.
- **English-medium teaching** with strong support services for international students under the Code of Practice.

### H3 — Major Intakes

New Zealand universities run two main intakes — **February** (the primary intake) and **July** (a strong secondary intake for many programmes). A **November** intake exists at some Institutes of Technology and Polytechnics for selected courses. Apply 4–6 months before your intended start date.

### H3 — Entry Requirements

**Undergraduate**

- Minimum 60% in 12-year schooling.
- IELTS 6.0 overall with no band below 5.5 (6.5 with no band below 6.0 for top programmes).

**Postgraduate**

- Bachelor's degree from a recognised university.
- IELTS 6.5 overall with no band below 6.0 (7.0 for Teaching, Nursing, and Social Work).
- Statement of Purpose and academic references.

### H3 — Top Universities in New Zealand

University of Auckland · University of Otago · Victoria University of Wellington · University of Canterbury · Massey University · University of Waikato · Auckland University of Technology (AUT) · Lincoln University · Unitec Institute of Technology · Toi Ohomai Institute of Technology · Wellington Institute of Technology · Otago Polytechnic · Eastern Institute of Technology · Ara Institute of Canterbury · Manukau Institute of Technology.

### H3 — New Zealand Student Visa Process for Nepalese Students

1. **Receive your Offer of Place** from an NZQA-approved education provider after accepting and paying the tuition deposit.
2. **Apply for NOC** at noc.moest.gov.np.
3. **Complete the medical examination** at IOM Kathmandu (Panel Physician network).
4. **Police clearance** from your local district administration office for applicants over 17.
5. **Prepare financial evidence** — minimum NZ$ 20,000 per year living expenses plus full first-year tuition.
6. **Submit your Fee Paying Student visa application** online via Immigration New Zealand (immigration.govt.nz) with Offer of Place, medical, police clearance, financial proof, and English test results.
7. **Pay the visa fee** (currently NZ$ 750).
8. **Biometrics** if requested at VFS Kathmandu.
9. **Decision** — typical processing time is 6–8 weeks. Approved visas are issued electronically.

### H3 — Frequently Asked Questions about Studying in New Zealand

**Q: How many hours can international students work in New Zealand?**
A: Student visa holders can generally work up to 20 hours per week during the academic term and full-time during scheduled course breaks. PhD and master's research students can work full-time throughout their study.

**Q: How long can I stay in New Zealand after graduation?**
A: The Post-Study Work Visa allows 1, 2, or 3 years of open work rights after qualifying study, depending on the level of qualification and the location of study. A bachelor's or postgraduate qualification (Level 7 or higher, at least 30 weeks) qualifies for up to 3 years.

**Q: What is the minimum bank balance required for a New Zealand student visa?**
A: You must show NZ$ 20,000 per year for living costs (or NZ$ 1,667 per month for stays under one year), plus full payment of the first year's tuition or an arrangement to pay it. Funds should be in a bank account in your name, a sponsor's, or held by an authorised provider.

## 5. Ireland

**Page:** `/ireland/` · **Post ID:** 1880 · **Slug:** `ireland`

### H1 — Study in Ireland

### H2 — Overview

Ireland is one of Europe's fastest-growing study destinations and the only English-speaking country in the European Union. For Nepalese students, Ireland offers globally ranked universities, a strong technology and pharmaceuticals job market (Google, Meta, Apple, Microsoft, Pfizer all have European headquarters in Dublin), the Third Level Graduate Programme that allows two years of post-study work for master's graduates, and lower tuition than the UK or USA at most institutions.

### H2 — Why Study in Ireland

- **English-speaking EU country** — degrees recognised across Europe.
- **Third Level Graduate Programme** — 2 years post-study work for master's, 1 year for bachelor's, on Stamp 1G.
- **Major tech and pharma hub** — Dublin hosts EU HQs for the world's largest tech and pharmaceutical companies.
- **High-quality universities** — Trinity College Dublin, UCD, and University of Galway in the QS top 300.
- **Friendly visa policy** for genuine students — Stamp 2 student permission.

### H3 — Major Intakes

Ireland has two main intakes — **September** (the primary intake) and **January** (a smaller intake at most universities, primarily for select master's programmes). Apply 6 months before your intended start date.

### H3 — Entry Requirements

**Undergraduate**

- Minimum 60% in 12-year schooling.
- IELTS 6.0–6.5 overall (no band below 5.5–6.0).

**Postgraduate**

- Bachelor's degree from a recognised university with at least 60% (2:2 Irish equivalent).
- IELTS 6.5 overall with no band below 6.0 (7.0 for some teaching and clinical programmes).
- Statement of Purpose and academic references.

### H3 — Top Universities in Ireland

Trinity College Dublin · University College Dublin (UCD) · University of Galway (formerly NUI Galway) · University College Cork (UCC) · Dublin City University (DCU) · Maynooth University · University of Limerick · Technological University Dublin (TU Dublin) · Munster Technological University · Atlantic Technological University · South East Technological University · Royal College of Surgeons in Ireland (RCSI) · National College of Ireland · Griffith College · Dublin Business School.

### H3 — Ireland Student Visa Process for Nepalese Students

1. **Receive your unconditional Letter of Acceptance** from a programme on the Irish Naturalisation and Immigration Service (INIS) Interim List of Eligible Programmes (ILEP).
2. **Pay your full first-year tuition fee** to the institution and obtain a payment receipt — usually required upfront for non-EU students.
3. **Apply for NOC** at noc.moest.gov.np.
4. **Prepare financial evidence** — €10,000 (currently) in your name covering living costs, plus tuition receipt.
5. **Get private medical insurance** covering the duration of stay.
6. **Submit your D Study visa application** online at the Irish Immigration Service portal (irishimmigration.ie).
7. **Submit your passport and supporting documents** to VFS Kathmandu within the required timeframe — pay the visa fee (currently €60).
8. **Decision** — typical processing time is 8 weeks. After arrival, register with the Garda National Immigration Bureau (GNIB) for your Stamp 2 residence permit.

### H3 — Frequently Asked Questions about Studying in Ireland

**Q: How many hours can international students work in Ireland?**
A: Students on Stamp 2 (full-time degree at an ILEP-listed programme) can work up to 20 hours per week during term time and 40 hours per week during scheduled holiday periods (May–August and 15 December–15 January).

**Q: What is the Third Level Graduate Programme in Ireland?**
A: The Third Level Graduate Programme (Stamp 1G) allows non-EU graduates of Level 8 (bachelor's) programmes to remain in Ireland for 12 months to seek employment, and Level 9 or 10 (master's or PhD) graduates to remain for up to 24 months.

**Q: How much money do I need to show for an Ireland student visa from Nepal?**
A: You must show evidence of €10,000 in your bank account (or a sponsor's) for living expenses for one year, plus proof that the first year's tuition has been paid in full to the Irish institution.

## 6. Italy

**Page:** `/italy/` · **Post ID:** 1886 · **Slug:** `italy`

### H1 — Study in Italy

### H2 — Overview

Italy combines world-class universities, low public-university tuition (often €1,000–€4,000 per year for non-EU students), strong DSU/INPS scholarships and fee waivers, and full European Union mobility after graduation. For Nepalese students, Italy is increasingly attractive — degrees are Bologna-Process aligned (recognised across Europe), most master's programmes offer English-medium tracks especially in business, engineering, and design, and graduates can convert their student permit into a 12-month job-search permit after completion.

### H2 — Why Study in Italy

- **Low public university tuition** — €1,000–€4,000 per year typical, with means-tested reductions for low-income families.
- **DSU regional scholarships** — most regions offer scholarships covering tuition + accommodation + meal vouchers for eligible international students.
- **English-medium master's programmes** — over 500 master's programmes now taught fully in English.
- **Bologna-Process degrees** — recognised across the European Higher Education Area.
- **Post-study job-search permit** — 12-month extension after graduation to find work in Italy.

### H3 — Major Intakes

Italy's main intake is **September/October** — the academic year aligns with the European calendar. A few master's programmes offer a **February/March** intake. Public universities require a pre-enrolment process via Universitaly (universitaly.it) typically completed between May and July; deadlines are strict.

### H3 — Entry Requirements

**Undergraduate**

- Minimum 60% in 12-year schooling and Declaration of Value (DoV) from the Italian Embassy in New Delhi (currently the consular jurisdiction for Nepal).
- IELTS 6.0 overall (for English-taught programmes) or B2 Italian (CILS / CELI / PLIDA) for Italian-taught programmes.
- For some programmes (Architecture, Medicine), an entrance test is required.

**Postgraduate**

- Bachelor's degree from a recognised university.
- IELTS 6.5 overall (English-taught) or B2 Italian (Italian-taught).
- Statement of Purpose and academic references; portfolio for design/architecture programmes.

### H3 — Top Universities in Italy

University of Bologna · Sapienza University of Rome · University of Padua · University of Milan · Polytechnic University of Milan · University of Pisa · University of Florence · University of Turin · University of Naples Federico II · Politecnico di Torino · University of Trento · Bocconi University (private) · LUISS Guido Carli (private) · IULM University · University of Genoa · Ca' Foscari University of Venice · University of Bari · University of Verona · University of Parma · University of Pavia.

### H3 — Italy Student Visa (Type D) Process for Nepalese Students

1. **Pre-enrolment via Universitaly** — register and select your programme between May and July (for September intake).
2. **Receive your Letter of Acceptance** from the Italian university.
3. **Apply for NOC** at noc.moest.gov.np.
4. **Apply for Declaration of Value (DoV) / Statement of Verification** at the Italian Embassy in New Delhi for your Nepalese qualifications.
5. **Pay your tuition fee** (where required upfront — many public universities allow payment after enrolment in Italy).
6. **Prepare financial evidence** — minimum €6,500 per year in your name or a sponsor's.
7. **Get health insurance** covering the duration of stay (€150/year typical).
8. **Submit your long-stay study visa (Type D) application** at the Italian Embassy in New Delhi via VFS — passport, Letter of Acceptance, DoV, financial proof, accommodation proof, insurance.
9. **Visa fee** (€50–€60). **Decision** — typically 30–60 days.
10. **After arrival in Italy** — apply for the Permesso di Soggiorno per Studio (residence permit for study) within 8 working days at the local post office and Questura.

### H3 — Frequently Asked Questions about Studying in Italy

**Q: Do I need to speak Italian to study in Italy?**
A: Not for English-taught programmes — over 500 master's and many bachelor's programmes are taught fully in English, especially in business, engineering, design, and economics. For Italian-taught programmes you must demonstrate B2 Italian (CILS, CELI, or PLIDA certificate).

**Q: What scholarships are available for Nepalese students in Italy?**
A: The biggest are DSU regional scholarships (Diritto allo Studio Universitario), means-tested by family income and merit, which can cover tuition + accommodation + meals. Other options include Italian government scholarships, individual university scholarships, and Erasmus Mundus joint programmes for master's-level study.

**Q: How many hours can international students work in Italy?**
A: Student visa holders can work up to 20 hours per week during academic terms and full-time during scheduled vacation periods, on a Permesso di Soggiorno for study, totalling a maximum of 1,040 hours per year.

## 7. South Korea

**Page:** `/south-korea/` (formerly `/south-korea-2/`) · **Post ID:** 7130 · **Slug:** `south-korea`

### H1 — Study in South Korea

### H2 — Overview

South Korea has emerged as one of Asia's fastest-rising study destinations, backed by world-class universities, government-funded scholarships through the Global Korea Scholarship (GKS) programme, and a strong post-graduation job market in technology, engineering, and Korean cultural industries. For Nepalese students, South Korea offers high-quality engineering and business programmes, scholarships covering full tuition and living costs, and a D-2 student visa with part-time work rights and a clear pathway to D-10 (job-seeking) and E-7 (work) visas after graduation.

### H2 — Why Study in South Korea

- **Top engineering and tech universities** — Seoul National, KAIST, POSTECH, Yonsei, and Korea University are in the QS Asia top 50.
- **Global Korea Scholarship (GKS)** — full tuition + monthly stipend + return airfare, open to international students.
- **Strong post-study career options** — D-10 job-seeker visa for 6+ months after graduation, conversion to E-7 work visa with a job offer.
- **English-taught programmes** — most universities offer English-medium master's and PhD programmes in business, engineering, and policy.
- **Cultural exchange** — strong support services for international students and active Korean Wave (K-content) industry.

### H3 — Major Intakes

South Korea's main intake is **March** (the start of the Korean academic year). A strong secondary intake runs in **September** for most programmes. Korean language programmes have four intakes (March, June, September, December). GKS scholarship applications open in February–March (graduate) and August–September (undergraduate) the year before intake.

### H3 — Entry Requirements

**Undergraduate**

- Minimum 70% in 12-year schooling.
- IELTS 5.5–6.0 OR TOPIK Level 3+ (for Korean-taught programmes).
- Statement of Purpose and a study plan.

**Postgraduate**

- Bachelor's degree from a recognised university with GPA 3.0/4.0 or equivalent.
- IELTS 6.0–6.5 OR TOPIK Level 4–5 (Korean-taught).
- Two academic references and a research proposal (for research master's and PhD).

### H3 — South Korea Student Visa (D-2) Process for Nepalese Students

1. **Receive your Certificate of Admission** from a Korean university and pay any deposit required.
2. **Apply for NOC** at noc.moest.gov.np.
3. **Health check** — TB test certificate from IOM Kathmandu (mandatory for visa applicants from Nepal).
4. **Prepare financial evidence** — minimum US$ 20,000 in your bank account (or sponsor's) covering living and tuition costs.
5. **Apostille your academic documents** and obtain official transcripts and certificates from CDC Bhaktapur or Tribhuvan University as required.
6. **Apply for the D-2 visa** at the Embassy of the Republic of Korea in Kathmandu — passport, Certificate of Admission, financial proof, sponsor affidavit, academic transcripts, TB clearance.
7. **Pay the visa fee** (currently US$ 60) and submit biometrics at the embassy.
8. **Decision** — typical processing time is 2–4 weeks.
9. **After arrival in Korea** — register at the local immigration office within 90 days for an Alien Registration Card (ARC).

### H3 — Frequently Asked Questions about Studying in South Korea

**Q: Do I need to know Korean to study in South Korea?**
A: Not for English-taught programmes, which are widely available at master's and PhD level and increasingly at undergraduate level in business, engineering, and international studies. Korean-taught programmes require TOPIK Level 3 or 4 (intermediate). Many universities also offer one year of preparatory Korean language study before starting your degree.

**Q: What is the Global Korea Scholarship (GKS)?**
A: GKS is the South Korean government's flagship scholarship for international students. It covers full tuition, a monthly living stipend (currently 900,000–1,000,000 KRW), one year of Korean language training, return airfare, settlement allowance, and medical insurance. Applications open annually through Korean embassies and partner universities.

**Q: Can D-2 student visa holders work part-time in South Korea?**
A: Yes — after six months on a D-2 visa, students can apply for part-time work permission (S-3 work code) and work up to 20 hours per week during term time and unlimited hours during vacations. Specific permission must be obtained from the local immigration office.
